# ADR-0057: The populated world is a deterministic fixture generated once, served as evidence through a port, and provably absent from production

**Status:** Accepted
**Date:** 2026-08-11
**Deciders:** Founding architect, front-end parity plan prompt 4
**Relates to:** Charter non-negotiables #1, #2, #3, #4, #5, #7, #10; ADR-0018 (line budgets),
ADR-0022 (derived-artifact provenance), ADR-0024 (Salesforce deferred pending sandbox),
ADR-0027 (Wave 0 labeled fakes), ADR-0052 (the replay corpus, whose derivation discipline this reuses)
**Informed by:** `data/min-iris-gap-s4/report.md` (ranked "it is populated" the single largest cause
of the reference product feeling more capable), `docs/product-guide.md`, `docs/demo-design-language.md`

## Context

Verin's surfaces render mostly empty. The console lists whatever the walking skeleton happened to
create; every other surface either shows the Smiths or shows nothing. An empty product cannot feel
capable, and - more importantly for this build - an empty product cannot be *inspected*: nobody can
tell whether a household surface handles a blended family, a trust that pays a different household,
or four households called Smith, because there has never been more than one household to look at.

Three constraints shape the answer.

1. **Charter #3 forbids unlabeled synthetic data, end to end.** A hundred invented households is the
   largest body of synthetic data this repository will hold. Every record must be labeled, and every
   figure computed from those records must be a watermarked demonstration artifact (ADR-0022).
2. **Charter #2 forbids speculative modeling.** Holdings, beneficiary designations, authorized
   signers, and bank instructions are owned by custodians and the CRM of record. They are *evidence*
   that the v3 architecture routes through an `EvidenceSource`, not records Verin's house CRM
   produces. Adding nine tables to the house CRM to hold them would model the wrong thing and would
   have to be unwound when the real adapter lands.
3. **A demonstration world that could reach production is a liability, not a feature.** The
   clean-slate guarantee has to be *provable*, not promised.

## Decision

### 1. One generator, reusing the corpus machinery

The world is generated by `scripts/world/**` from two hand-owned spec files
(`fixtures/world/spec/{roster,featured}.json`) and one root seed. It reuses the replay corpus's
derivation primitives directly - `scripts/corpus/seed.ts` (SHA-256 keyed by seed + path + field, never
a stream PRNG), `scripts/corpus/clock.ts` (instant arithmetic with no wall clock), `_util.ts`,
`strict-json.ts`, `tree.ts` - rather than inventing a second mechanism. Path-keying means adding a
household changes exactly that household's bytes.

`pnpm world:generate` writes `fixtures/world/manifest.json` and `fixtures/world/households/*.json`.
`pnpm world:validate` regenerates from the spec and **byte-compares** against the committed tree, so a
hand edit fails CI - the same discipline, and the same runner/fence split, as `corpus`.

### 2. Ten hand-authored, ninety derived, one materializer

`featured.json` carries genuine hand-written structure and prose for the ten households the product
surfaces most - including the deliberately awkward ones: four Smith households, a blended family whose
beneficiary designations do not mirror, a trust registered in one household that pays another, an
entity household with no natural-person client, and a household whose evidence is deliberately stale.
The other ninety are drawn path-keyed **into the same draft shape**, and a single materializer turns
every draft into the same typed household. The ninety are therefore never structurally thinner than
the ten, only less specific - and each household states which it is, on its own surface.

**Amended by D-198** (review round). Two of the three account rules the earlier rounds recorded were
checked on the generated output; the third - an entity household's people hold no PERSONAL accounts
inside it - existed only as a filter inside the derived generator, so four normative artifacts claimed
a rule no gate could fail on. It is now enforced in `accountRuleProblems` as the predicate an output
can be held to (nobody the household records ONLY as an authorized signer appears in any account's
`ownerKeys`), and it holds the hand-authored ten as well as the derived ninety. A generator filter is
not a check: a hand-authored household reaches the same output through another door.

**Amended by D-198.** A household's `evidence` block carries the PROVENANCE of each class rather than a
bare instant. The materializer measures each observation against the world's `asOf` once, and every
record of that class - the account balance, the lots inside it, the beneficiary designation, and the
evidence line the detail surface prints - carries that exact value, so one instant can no longer
produce two confidences on one page.

### 3. The world is EVIDENCE, behind a port

`HouseholdWorldSource` (`src/domain/world/household-world.ts`) is the port; the fixture adapter
(`src/infrastructure/world/fixture-world-source.ts`) is its Wave 0 implementation under ADR-0027. It
is **replaced**, not relabeled, when a real `EvidenceSource` lands (ADR-0024's un-defer trigger).

The house CRM keeps only what it genuinely owns. `seedWorldIntoCrm` projects households, their people,
and their open items into `households` / `contacts` / `tasks`; it deliberately does **not** seed
financial accounts, because an account in the house CRM is the output of the account-opening flow and
custodial positions are evidence. The CRM remains the authority on *which* households a tenant may
see - the household surface authorizes through a tenant-scoped repository read and only then asks the
port for depth.

**Amended by D-198** (review round). The loud refusal D-196 added tested the SYMPTOM - the load wrote
nothing - which the dangerous case (another org holds the seed-derived ids) and a perfectly safe one
(this org already holds an earlier load of the same world) both produce. Because household ids are
stable across world content while the digest is not, that broke `pnpm db:seed` on the next world
regeneration anyone made. The refusal now tests the CONDITION: a conflicting household held by a
DIFFERENT org refuses loudly and by name; a same-org re-offer writes whatever is genuinely new and
reports honest counts.

**Amended by D-199** (review round). A cross-household link's counterparty is authorized through the
same tenant-scoped read as the subject, and an unauthorized one is now withheld WHOLE: the view model
receives no entry for it and emits an opaque page-local ordinal in place of the name, the world key
and the link. A world key is `<surname>-<given name>`, so the earlier fallback disclosed the party at
lower fidelity while the test that guarded it - the exact display name is absent - still passed. The
hand-written prose that named the same party in three other fields is corrected with it, and
`crossHouseholdProseProblems` holds both authors to that for good.

### 4. Health is computed, never stored

`computeHouseholdHealth` (`src/domain/world/health.ts`) is a pure, clock-free, integer-only
computation over six weighted factors: liquidity, evidence freshness, instruction integrity,
beneficiary completeness, authority currency, and operational load. Each factor returns its score, its
weight, a sentence a person can act on, and the records it read. **The generator is forbidden from
emitting a health field at all** (the `world-provenance` fence fails on one), because a stored score
is a typed number wearing a computation's clothes.

Every health figure is published through `deriveArtifactProvenance`, so it renders watermarked
"demonstration - not a compliance record" and `canFeedComplianceDecision` refuses it (ADR-0022).

**Amended by D-197** (review round). Beneficiary materiality was ONE set answering two different
questions, and it got the surface wrong in both directions in turn: scoring every registration
invented a deficiency on a third of the accounts, and then wording the empty panel from the health set
told a reader that an individual or joint account cannot take a transfer-on-death designation. The
questions are now two named sets in the shared world vocabulary -
`BENEFICIARY_CAPABLE_REGISTRATIONS` (can this registration carry a designation at all?) and
`BENEFICIARY_SCORED_REGISTRATIONS` (does a missing designation count against health?) - and neither
answers the other's. The health factor and the deficiency note read the second; the empty beneficiary
panel reads the first. An absence is never reported as a gap, and a capability is never denied.

### 5. The clean-slate guarantee is counted, not promised

Every world row is written with `prov_source = 'fixture'` - not `verin-crm`. `scripts/fixture-purge.ts`
derives the swept table list **from the shipped DDL** (any table whose DDL carries a `prov_source`
column) and counts synthetic-sourced rows across every tenant; `pnpm fixture:check` fails on the first
one. A sweep that finds no provenance-bearing table reports a problem rather than passing vacuously
(charter #4). The fixture adapter additionally refuses to serve anything under `APP_ENV=production`,
so the guarantee is structural on both sides.

The derivation is read three ways that share no code, and any disagreement is a sweep problem: a
structural parse of each table's balanced body and its top-level column items; a plain count of every
`prov_source` the DDL names; and the store's OWN column catalog, which is not a reading of the DDL at
all and therefore also catches a provenance-bearing table created outside `MIGRATION_SQL`. Two
readings that resolve a declaration the same way agree by construction and cross-check nothing.

**Amended by D-196 and D-197** (review rounds). The second reading counts DECLARATIONS, not mentions:
a column-level `CHECK (prov_source IN (...))` names the column twice for one declaration, and
`CREATE INDEX ... ON t (prov_source)` is the index this cross-tenant sweep would itself want, so
counting mentions failed the check on ordinary schema work while naming an unswept table that does not
exist. Declaration-versus-reference is decided off the CLOSED set of column types a provenance column
is declared as (`PROVENANCE_COLUMN_TYPES`), never off a list of keywords that may follow a reference:
that list is open and grows with SQL usage, and every keyword nobody thought of would false-fail the
check the same way. A declaration naming a type outside the closed set fails the other way and says
so, so the reading can never go quietly blind. The third reading matches
`ANY(current_schemas(false))`, the search path the sweep's own unqualified `SELECT` resolves through -
`current_schema()` alone was NARROWER than the sweep, the fail-open direction this module refuses -
while the view and other-schema false alarms stay closed through the `BASE TABLE` join. A false alarm
on the clean-slate check is as corrosive as a false pass: it is the one check that has to be
unambiguous.

### 6. Budgets are amended by measurement

`src/domain/**` gains the world model and the health computation; `src/infrastructure/**` gains the
fixture adapter and the CRM projection; `scripts/**` gains the generator. ADR-0018's per-layer
ceilings are raised against figures **re-measured on the tree as it lands**:

| Layer | Measured | Ceiling | Named headroom |
|---|---|---|---|
| `domain` | 5,045 | 5,150 | 105 |
| `infrastructure` | 8,144 | 8,250 | 106 |
| `tooling` (`scripts/**`) | 14,005 | 14,200 | 195 |

**Amended by D-194** (review round). The figures are re-taken on the tree as it lands: the
clean-slate sweep's third, catalog-based reading, the two account rules `validateWorld` now holds
both authors to, and the roster's asset-class uniqueness refinement left `tooling` with 15 lines of
headroom against the original 14,000 - the condition ADR-0018's own commentary calls out, where the
next one-line correction fails an unrelated ceiling. `tooling` moves to 14,200; `domain` and
`infrastructure` are unmoved and merely re-measured.

**Re-measured by D-196 and D-197** (review rounds), with NO ceiling moving in either: the pairing is
5,150 / 8,250 / 14,200 against 5,079 / 8,184 / 14,101 as this round lands - 71, 66 and 99 lines of
correction room, named rather than banked. A ceiling carrying a stale measurement is a ceiling nobody
re-took.

`contracts` and `presentation` are unmoved. Headroom is *named* rather than banked, so the next change
to any of these layers reads as the measured amendment it is. No ceiling here was paid for by deleting
documentation or folding readable code onto fewer lines (ADR-0048/0049/0050).

## Alternatives rejected

**Nine new house-CRM tables for holdings, beneficiaries, signers, bank instructions, withdrawals,
restrictions, pending actions and activity.** This is what "seed the house CRM with the populated
world" reads like on first pass, and the charter's system-of-record section can be read to require it.
It was rejected because it models the wrong owner: charter #2 says model only what the declared flows
require, and no flow reads or writes a custodial position today. The v3 architecture routes exactly
this data through `EvidenceSource`, so the tables would be built to be deleted.

**A client-side world.** Explicitly a DO-NOT-PORT disease ("the demo shadow-world pattern"). The world
is read server-side, behind authorization, and reaches the browser only as a built view model.

**Extending the replay corpus to a hundred households.** The corpus is a separate normative artifact
with captain-signed, digest-bound signoff (ADR-0052); regeneration invalidates that signoff. Growing
it to carry demo population would conflate "does Verin catch this defect?" with "does the product look
alive?" and would destroy a signature to do it.

**Storing the health score in the fixture.** Faster to render and impossible to defend. The plan's own
words are that the numbers must "read as earned rather than typed"; a stored score is typed.

**Adding `sep-ira` and `education-529` to the house CRM's `AccountType`.** Would have let the CRM
projection carry every registration type, at the cost of offering to open a 529 in a flow that does
not support one. The projection drops accounts instead, and the world remains authoritative on
registration.

## Trade-offs

**Gained.** A hundred inspectable households, each with people and relationships, entities, accounts
with holdings and registration types, beneficiaries and signers, bank instructions with change
history, planned withdrawals, restrictions and standing instructions, pending actions, recent
activity, and a health score that explains itself. Byte-identical regeneration under any timezone. A
clean-slate guarantee that is counted from the shipped DDL rather than asserted.

**Sacrificed.** The world's depth is not queryable SQL: a future "every household with an unverified
bank instruction" report has to read the port, not the store, until the real evidence adapter lands.
Directory assembly loads every household file on first request (cached thereafter), which is
acceptable at a hundred and would not be at ten thousand.

## Consequences

- `pnpm db:seed` now loads the world; e2e and the audit-chain gate run against a populated store.
- `/app/households` and `/app/households/[key]` are new authenticated routes, bound to the axe scan.
- `world` joins the blocking CI jobs beside `corpus` and `golden-cases`.
- The `world-determinism`, `world-provenance`, and `clean-slate` fences ship with their adversarial
  companions and proof-log entries (charter #1/#4).

## Revisit When

- **A real `EvidenceSource` lands** (ADR-0024's sandbox trigger). The fixture adapter is deleted, not
  relabeled, and the surfaces stop carrying their sample badges in the same PR.
- **The world exceeds ~500 households or the directory's first-request load exceeds 200ms.** The
  manifest-only path stops being enough and the summary rows need a store.
- **Any surface needs to query the world's depth across households.** That is the signal that the
  evidence belongs in a store behind a repository, and this ADR's "no new tables" decision is what
  should be reopened.
